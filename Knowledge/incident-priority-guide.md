# Enterprise IT Incident Priority Guide

## Purpose

This guide defines a consistent method for assigning incident priority based on business impact and urgency.

Priority must not be based only on technical severity.

## Priority Levels

### P1 - Critical

Use when:

- A business-critical service is completely unavailable.
- Multiple business units are unable to operate.
- There is a confirmed major security incident.
- Significant financial, regulatory, or safety impact is occurring.

Expected response:

- Immediate incident response.
- Incident manager engagement.
- Continuous stakeholder communication.
- Executive escalation where required.

### P2 - High

Use when:

- A major service is significantly degraded.
- A critical workflow is unavailable for a large user group.
- There is no practical workaround.
- The issue has significant business impact but is not a full enterprise outage.

Expected response:

- Urgent investigation.
- Assigned engineering ownership.
- Regular stakeholder updates.

### P3 - Medium

Use when:

- A limited group of users is affected.
- A workaround exists.
- Business operations can continue with reduced efficiency.

Expected response:

- Investigation during normal support processes.
- Resolution according to standard SLA.

### P4 - Low

Use when:

- Impact is minimal.
- The issue is cosmetic or informational.
- A workaround is available.
- There is no material business disruption.

Expected response:

- Schedule through normal support processes.

## Priority Assessment

Evaluate:

1. Number of users affected.
2. Business process affected.
3. Service criticality.
4. Availability of a workaround.
5. Financial or regulatory impact.
6. Time sensitivity.

## Important Rule

A technical error such as HTTP 500 or a failed workflow does not automatically mean P1.

Priority must be based on verified business impact.
